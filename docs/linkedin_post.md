I went looking for the six-day break this year and could not find it.

Every club complains about the short turnaround, so I expected it to show up
clearly. Across 2,291 AFL matches since 2015 it came back at +0.9 points of
margin, with a confidence interval sitting either side of zero. On this data a
side coming off five days and a side coming off nine are indistinguishable once
you know who they are playing and where.

The second one surprised me more. Playing at your own ground is worth about 1.75
points, and I cannot separate that from zero either. Having flown interstate
costs 5.8 points, and that one is solid. So home ground advantage in the AFL is
mostly not the ground. It is the other side's flight. Two Melbourne clubs at the
MCG is close to a neutral game.

Which leads somewhere I did not expect. A trip to Perth is the most expensive
journey on the schedule, 8.8 points to the visitor. West Coast and Fremantle
each flew ten times this season, more than any Victorian club, and they still
finish first and third on the travel ledger. They pay that cost one way.
Everyone who visits them pays more.

Across the league the draw was worth a spread of 135.8 points of margin between
the best and worst schedule, near enough to six points a game. That is enough to
move clubs several places, and the club-by-club version is in the app.

The part I want to be upfront about: replaying the last ten seasons, the side
this model called favourite won 2 flags out of 10 against 3.7 expected. Ten
seasons is not enough to convict it, but it is not a good look either, and
hiding it would make everything above worth less.

So, on the record before Saturday: Fremantle 55, Brisbane 45. Happy to be wrong
in public. 🙂
